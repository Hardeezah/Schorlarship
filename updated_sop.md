Statement of Purpose

I am interested in building machine learning systems that hold up outside the curated datasets they are trained on, specifically for deployment on embedded hardware in low-connectivity settings across Nigeria and West Africa, where the gap between benchmark accuracy and field performance has direct consequences for the people relying on the system.

I graduated with First Class Honours in Computer Science from Ahmadu Bello University, CGPA 4.53 out of 5.00, ranked the best female Computer Science graduate in my class. Since then I have worked as a founding engineer across several ventures. Below, I describe the two lines of work that have shaped my research interests, the mentorship that shaped how I approach them, and what I learned from each.

Edge Vision Under Data and Hardware Constraints. Crop disease detection is a domain where the constraints are structural: the model has to run on hardware a smallholder farmer can own, without a dependable connection back to a server. As part of a team at Ahmadu Bello University, I built CropDiseaseDetector (also entered under the name AgroGuardian), a combined hardware and mobile-app system addressing this directly [1].

1. Model architecture and training. I trained a MobileNetV2-based classifier, replacing its final layer to output 46 disease classes across ten crops (maize, tomato, rice, beans, cassava, soybeans, sorghum, wheat, potato, groundnut), with a softmax activation and the Adam optimizer, on a Kaggle dataset of roughly 46,753 images. Because the class distribution was uneven across crops, I applied SMOTE for data balancing during preprocessing rather than relying on the raw class frequencies. For real-time video, I separately trained a YOLOv8n detector on an augmented RoboFlow/Kaggle dataset (horizontal flip, blur, rotation), so that localization and fine-grained classification could each be optimized for its own task rather than compressing one architecture to the point of losing the distinctions between visually similar diseases. Both models run on an Orange Pi AIpro 20T with a Huawei Ascend chip under OpenEuler, fully offline, with a 7-inch touchscreen and multilingual text and audio support across nine languages. The classifier reached 98% accuracy in our evaluation. I have not yet had the training to evaluate whether that number holds up under distribution shift rather than on held-out data drawn from the same curated source, which is precisely the open question I describe below.

2. Extending the system to a second sensor modality. The same device integrates a soil health module: an NPK 7-in-1 sensor feeding a Random Forest model that gives farmers direct feedback on nutrient deficiencies. An earlier version of this module, developed independently, placed second in the Nigeria MATLAB Student Coding Challenge 2024 [2]. Combining vision and sensor-based inference on one embedded device, rather than treating them as separate products, taught me that the harder design problem in low-resource ML is rarely the model itself; it is deciding which signals justify a dedicated model versus which can share a pipeline without one degrading the other.

Research Mentorship. This work has been shaped by two mentors at Ahmadu Bello University. During a student internship in the Department of Computer Engineering, I worked under Dr. Zaharuddeen Haruna on control systems problems in MATLAB, in the same research area as his published work on linear quadratic regulator control for benchmark nonlinear systems such as the ball-and-beam and ball-on-sphere problems [3]. He later served as instructor for the CropDiseaseDetector team. In my subsequent industrial attachment period, I worked under Dr. Salisu Aliyu in the Department of Computer Science, whose research is in knowledge representation and reasoning; that exposure to structuring and grounding knowledge formally is part of why, in my later work described below, I built a retrieval layer rather than relying on a model's parametric knowledge alone.

Grounding Models in Regulatory Knowledge. At Koda, an AI trade compliance platform helping Nigerian SMEs navigate AfCFTA requirements, I built a vision model for HS code classification and a retrieval augmented compliance checker on FastAPI and pgvector. Generic classification fails here because customs documentation formats vary by SME and by product category, so I built the retrieval layer to ground classification decisions in the specific regulatory text rather than the model's parametric knowledge. I presented Koda at UNITAR's forum on Japan-Africa public-private partnership under TICAD9 [4], where the clearest feedback I received was that the compliance problem exists, in different forms, across every developing economy trying to formalize trade. I do not yet have benchmarked precision or recall for the compliance checker; what I have is manual spot-checking against real filings, which caught obvious errors but is not a substitute for a proper held-out evaluation set. That gap, between building something that works in the cases I happened to test and knowing rigorously where it fails, is itself the limitation I want graduate training to address.

Future directions. A curated Kaggle dataset, however large, does not capture the lighting, occlusion, and camera variation of images a farmer captures in the field, and I do not yet have a principled way to measure or close that gap; my 98% figure was measured on held-out data from the same source distribution, not on independently collected field images. I want to study adversarially learned domain generalization as a way to make that measurement, and the model, honest: can a classifier trained on a small, curated dataset be made to hold up under conditions it never saw in training, rather than only appearing robust because the test set resembles the training set? A related direction follows from Koda: SME compliance data is sensitive and cannot be pooled across companies or countries, which raises the question of how a shared model could be trained on distributed, siloed regulatory data using federated learning with differential privacy, without ever centralizing the underlying documents. I am currently developing a proposal that compares Dynamic Feature Recognition across image sequences against a single-image baseline for agricultural disease detection under embedded-device constraints, as a first, concrete step toward the first question.

At the University of Cambridge. I am particularly drawn to the work of Prof. Nic Lane and his Machine Learning Systems Lab (CaMLSys), specifically his recent research on scaling privacy-preserving AI through the open-source Flower federated learning framework. His focus on the systems-level bottlenecks of decentralized training connects directly to my interest in applying federated learning with differential privacy to siloed SME regulatory data, and my applied background in optimizing multi-modal models for constrained embedded devices (like the Orange Pi AIpro) would allow me to contribute practically to his lab's work on Edge AI. I am also highly interested in the research of Dr. Ferenc Huszár, specifically his recent work investigating the probabilistic foundations of out-of-distribution generalization and transferability. His theoretical focus on why deep networks generalize provides the exact rigorous framework I need to study adversarially learned domain generalization, and my experience building retrieval-augmented compliance checkers to ground models in regulatory text offers a practical, real-world testbed for his lab's research into robust, uncertainty-aware machine learning.

The thread across this work, from a soil and disease detection device built for smallholder farmers to a compliance platform used by real SMEs, is that I have already built the applied side of resource-constrained AI, under the mentorship of researchers working in control systems and knowledge representation. What I have not yet had is the research training to measure where these systems actually fail, rather than where I happened to test them, and to turn that into work that generalizes past the specific systems I have shipped. That is what I am asking a Ph.D. to give me.
