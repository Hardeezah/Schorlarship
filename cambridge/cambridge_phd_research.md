# Cambridge PhD in Computer Science: Direct from Bachelor's Route

Going directly from a Bachelor's degree to a PhD in Computer Science at the University of Cambridge is entirely possible, though it is highly competitive. Here is a detailed breakdown of the requirements and process.

## 🎓 Academic Requirements

> [!IMPORTANT]  
> **Master's Degree Not Strictly Mandatory**  
> While a Master's degree (like the MPhil in Advanced Computer Science) is highly desirable and serves as excellent preparation, the department explicitly states that a First Class-equivalent bachelor's degree can be sufficient for direct admission.

- **Degree Standard:** You must hold a **first-class honours degree** (or its international equivalent).
- **Relevant Fields:** A Computer Science degree is preferred, but applicants with a first-class degree in related numerate fields (Engineering, Science, Mathematics) are also strongly considered if they demonstrate significant preparation for doctoral-level CS research.
- **The Probationary Year:** All PhD students at Cambridge are initially admitted on a probationary basis, registered for the **Certificate of Postgraduate Study (CPGS)**. During your first year, you undertake research skills training, practical work, and write a research report. You are officially upgraded to full PhD status upon successful completion of the CPGS year.

## 📝 Application Requirements & Process

1. **Find a Supervisor:** This is arguably the most crucial step. You **must** identify and informally contact a potential supervisor via email before submitting your formal application to ensure your research interests align and they are willing to take you on.
2. **Research Proposal:** A clear, detailed proposal outlining the research work you intend to pursue. This is developed ideally in consultation with your proposed supervisor.
3. **Core Documents:**
   - Academic transcripts and degree certificates
   - Comprehensive CV/Resume
   - 2 Academic references (from your Bachelor's studies)
   - A sample of written work (if required by the specific research group)
4. **English Language Proficiency:** Evidence of proficiency (IELTS/TOEFL) is required if English is not your first language.
5. **Application Fee:** Required for the main university application.

# The Gates Cambridge Scholarship

The Gates Cambridge Scholarship is one of the most prestigious and competitive international scholarships in the world, covering the full cost of studying at Cambridge. 

## 🌍 Eligibility Requirements

- **Citizenship:** You must be a citizen of any country **outside the United Kingdom**.
- **Course Type:** You must be applying to pursue a full-time postgraduate degree (like a PhD).
- **Academic Background:** You must demonstrate outstanding academic excellence, which aligns perfectly with the university's First-Class honours requirement.

## 🎯 Core Selection Criteria

To be competitive, you must explicitly tailor your application to meet these four criteria:

1. **Outstanding intellectual ability**
2. **Reasons for choice of course:** Why this specific PhD, why this supervisor, and why Cambridge?
3. **A commitment to improving the lives of others:** This is the defining characteristic of the Gates scholarship. Your research and personal background must show a strong desire and track record of societal impact.
4. **Leadership potential:** Evidence that you can take others with you and drive change.

## 📅 Application Process & Timeline

> [!TIP]  
> **No Separate Application Form**  
> You apply for the Gates Cambridge Scholarship simultaneously with your PhD admission via the University’s Graduate Application Portal. You simply complete the specific Gates Cambridge funding section.

The timeline for Entry 2027/2028 is as follows:

- **US Citizens (Resident in the USA):**
  - **Applications Open:** September 2026
  - **Deadline:** Mid-October (typically around October 14, 2026)
  - **Outcome Notification:** By mid-December 2026

- **All Other Eligible Applicants (International Round):**
  - **Applications Open:** September 2026
  - **Deadline:** Course-dependent, but typically **early December 2026 to early January 2027**. You must adhere to the specific funding deadline for the Computer Science department.
  - **Outcome Notification:** Late March 2027

### Strategic Advice for Direct Bachelor's Applicants
Since you are bypassing a Master's degree, your **research proposal** and your **undergraduate research experience** need to be exceptionally strong. The Gates Cambridge committee will be looking for evidence that you are academically mature enough to jump straight into doctoral research while simultaneously possessing the leadership and social commitment the trust values.

# Funding: Airfare, Relocation & Stipends

Getting a fully funded PhD that covers **tuition, a living stipend, airfare, and relocation** is possible, but it depends entirely on the specific scholarship you secure.

## The Gates Cambridge Scholarship (Comprehensive Funding)
This is the most common path to getting *all* these costs covered. It provides:
- **University Composition Fee:** Full tuition coverage.
- **Maintenance Allowance (Stipend):** Approximately £21,000 – £22,050 per year for living costs.
- **Airfare:** One economy single airfare at both the beginning and end of the course.
- **Inbound Visa Costs:** Includes the costly Immigration Health Surcharge.

## Standard Funding (UKRI / Departmental / College)
Many other "fully funded" PhDs (such as those funded by UKRI or Cambridge Doctoral Studentships) will cover:
- **Tuition fees**
- **Maintenance Stipend:** Usually benchmarked around £21,805 per year.
- *Note:* These standard studentships typically **do not** cover airfare or relocation. The University’s official "Relocation Expenses Scheme" is strictly for newly appointed staff/employees, not PhD students.

# Navigating the Computer Science Faculty Directory

When looking for a direct PhD, you cannot just apply to the department generally; you must find a supervisor whose research aligns with yours and who is willing to take you on.

Here is how you should interpret and navigate the Cambridge Computer Science Faculty Directory (The Computer Laboratory):

## 1. Identify Research Groups & Themes
The faculty is organized into specific research groups (e.g., Artificial Intelligence, Computer Architecture, Graphics & Interaction, Security, Systems Research). 
- **What this means for you:** You need to identify which group(s) your research proposal fits into. You will apply to work within one of these groups.

## 2. Evaluate Individual Faculty Members (Supervisors)
Within each group's directory, you will find lists of Principal Investigators (PIs), Professors, and Lecturers.
- **What to look for:** Look at their recent publications and current projects. You are looking for a PI whose current trajectory matches your proposed PhD topic.
- **Direct Contact:** The directory provides their contact information. It is standard practice to email a potential supervisor *before* you apply. Send them a concise email with your CV and a summary of your research proposal, asking if they are taking on new PhD students and if they would be interested in your project.
